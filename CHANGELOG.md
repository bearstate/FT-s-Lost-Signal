# FT's Geology: Lost Signal 0.1.0

The first release, a beta. Forge 1.20.1 (47.2 or newer), with FT's Geology 0.11.4, Feel the Nature 1.2.0, Simple Voice
Chat 2.6 and the Simple Radio ham fork.

Every line is voiced: the District, the lost hikers (their breath and calls too), the keepers, the man on the radio who
is not who he says, and the stranger, by day and through the door at night. Subtitles show the words as well.

- **Lookouts with people in them**: empty, kept, abandoned in a hurry, or kept by the stranger; changing from dawn to
  dawn.
- **Keepers**: a name, a voice, a temper; talk to them, run their errands, earn their trust, stay the night. When he
  comes to the door a nervous keeper gets under the bed; tell any of them to hide. One still standing when the door
  gives is taken, and in the morning the lookout is one left in a hurry.
- **The rescue frequency**: the District's day, calls to and from the lookouts round you, the rescue panel (`J`).
  The District's own calls are answered from the panel: the radio check, the end of day check, and the weather, sent
  as the cab's instruments read it (FT's Geology's weather terminal); unanswered, it asks once more. What comes over
  the air is shown only where it reaches your set, by the radio's own propagation: behind the hills a voice is static,
  or nothing; the panel lists the lookouts round with their S-meter readings, the ones out of reach last. Away from
  the station, a handheld transceiver on 145.500 (in a hand or on the hotbar) opens the panel too, as far as its five
  watts carry; a PMR walkie-talkie cannot be set there, and you are told so.
- **Lost hikers**: guided in by radio, by the fire finder's bearings, by flares, smoke and the signal lamp. Found,
  they are talked to face to face: told to follow, to wait, or the way to the lookout; they say so when they cannot
  follow where you went. Come in, they go up the stair and in at the cab's door for the night, and down again in the
  morning. A lamp waved at night is a bright swinging light seen from far off, the lantern in their hand; by day
  they say you would never see it. The night's monsters hunt them as they hunt villagers and they run from them; one
  killed is lost, the scream heard on the mountain, what is left of them where it happened (hikers.monsters).
- **Lost yourself**: out on the mountain with a handheld, the panel lists the lookouts round by how the set hears
  them (which way they lie is what you do not know). Called, a keeper puts a flare up from the lookout to walk
  towards, lights the lamp by night, and asks for one back; seen from the cab, it is taken a bearing on and the way in
  told. A flare, or a lantern swung at night, seen from a lookout with no call is answered with a flare from it. From
  a lookout no one keeps, someone answers too, and comes down for you.
- **Flares of your own**: the keepers', the crates' and the camps' flares go up when used, as the hikers' do; paper,
  gunpowder, redstone and string make two. The old ones, rockets by another name, become flares in the hand. A
  lantern used at the air is swung over your head, a light seen from far off at night.
- **The lookout terminal**: a 1994 tube monitor and its keyboard on the desk, run off the station's power (with the
  generator cold, the screen is dark). The District's messages; the duty roster (the morning radio check, the one
  o'clock fire weather observation, the end of day check, the walkway lamp at dusk); the observation itself, read off
  the instruments by eye and typed in by hand, the District holding each figure against what the instruments truly
  read and sending it back where too many are off; the smoke report, its bearing and distance typed off the fire
  finder; orders; and the keeper's record. Worked from the keyboard alone, too.
- **Marion on the night desk**: after dark the District's night desk writes to your terminal, a night at a time:
  who she is, what you did that day, the knock at your door, the friend she lost in 1996, and an old lookout called
  Hollow Peak. Some of it can be written back to, one of two ways (A or B on the terminal), and she answers.
- **Posted for the season**: at a lookout the District posts you there, says so on the radio and on the terminal, and
  sends the terminal, the lookout guide and a page someone left folded in it up with the next crate. In a world that
  had lookouts before, nothing is set into a lookout someone has been living in. Each lookout answers the District's
  radio checks for itself.
- **Pay credit**: weekly, for each day on duty and each duty done, and for good work (a hiker brought in, a fire called
  in, a report right to a degree, an observation right in every reading); spent on orders from the terminal (coal,
  food, flares, a lantern, a compass, a spyglass, a signal lamp, a terminal, a handheld, a district map with the
  lookout in its middle and every other lookout known marked on it), sent up with the next
  morning's crate. A crate still standing at the foot of the trail is filled rather than another set beside it.
- **The season**: opening, the fire season, closing, the lookouts shut for the winter; by Serene Seasons' seasons where
  it is installed, else a calendar of five weeks of our own. The duties and the pay keep to it.
- **Fire danger**: rated each morning by the District from the last one o'clock observation (how dry and warm, the
  wind, the days since rain), sent to every terminal, and the fires go by it: a dry afternoon in extreme danger burns
  ten times as often as one in low. A fire danger sign for the foot of the trail, its arrow set each morning.
- **Lightning**: in a thunderstorm a lookout is struck once or twice, through its rod, after a warning (the radio
  crackles, the rod hums and glows blue). On the glass-footed lightning stool, only the flash; on the floor, thrown,
  dazed and hurt, but never left with less than two hearts (`lookouts.lightning`).
- **The crews**: a crew is sent to every fire called in, on the radio as it sets out; by night its headlamps are seen
  from the lookout, a file of lights coming up the valley, then spread round the fire's edge. Down in the trees they
  cannot raise the District and ask the lookout to pass it on: a relay, answered from the panel, and a little to the
  keeper's credit. On the radio again once it is out (lines subtitled until recorded).
- **A compass in the spyglass**: looked through, a tape of bearings across the top of the view, so a smoke or a flare
  is read from the walkway, or out on the mountain.
- **Letters from the found**: a few days after a hiker is brought in, a letter from them comes up with the crate, in
  their own words, and something with it: cookies, a cake, a compass, a photograph of the summit, a cheque for the
  lookouts' fund, or now and then a pup, waiting by the crate.
- **Dogs know him**: a tame dog growls at the stranger when he is near and looks his way, wherever he stands, before
  anyone sees him.
- **Rank**: seasonal lookout, lookout, senior lookout, district lookout, by service (duties done, fires called in,
  hikers brought in, right observations, nights he went away): a raise with each, and more of the order list.
- **Missing**: a hiker lost from a lookout and never found is posted up at the foot of its trail, a sign facing down
  the slope: MISSING, their name, the day they were last heard, the frequency.
- **The season's evaluation**: as the lookouts close, days on duty, the share of the roster done, fires and hikers,
  a grade, a bonus for a good one, and whether you are wanted back.
- **Advancements**: a Lookout tab, from the first posting to the night he went away from your door.
- **One thing at a time**: no one says a line over their own last one; the stranger is only ever in one place.
- **Older lookouts made safe**: one built before Feel the Nature brought its stair out onto the walkway gets a hatch
  over the stairwell in its floor, and lanterns under it; and every lookout is kept off by FT's Geology's earthquakes
  where its API 5 offers it. After a quake felt round the lookouts the District asks after them, answered from the panel.
- **Forest fires** in forest country, seen far off: smoke from a few places across it, dark off the burning and
  greying as it rises, leaning and drifting on FT's Geology's wind, an orange glow under it at night, flames close to
  that burn nothing. Read off the fire finder, reported and crossed. Fire really burning round a lookout (lightning,
  a campfire got away, another mod's wildfires) is found too, and the crew sent to it puts it out.
- **Supply crates** at the foot of the trail.
- **The stranger**: by day a man with things to say; by night the mask, the door, the window. He comes up to the
  lookout on foot: up the trail, up the stair, along the walkway, seen from the windows if you look (and he looks back).
  Masked, he can be fought, and does not run; driven off, he does not fall but goes back into the dark, and comes again
  sooner and worse. Out of the cab when he comes, he comes for you where you are (quicker when you do not look, and a
  drop between you does not keep him off for long); back behind the door, he is at the door again. Hiding under the
  bed, looking out from under it at the room.
- **The rules hold**: the guide's and the page folded in it are true. A lookout with its lamp lit is one someone is
  up in, and he is less sure of it; a dark one is an empty one to him, and he comes to see. Give him water by day and he
  takes it as an invitation, that night and the next. Leave the cab's door open and there is no knock: he is in.
- **What a duty let go costs**: miss the end of day check and at two in the morning "the District" calls your lookout
  and asks if you are alone up there (Marion calls you by your name; this calls you Lookout). Miss the one o'clock
  observation and in the morning one is on your terminal that you never sent. Let a fire burn itself out uncalled and
  someone thanks you for it on the radio. And now and then, at night, a message from no one (`desk.horror`).
- **The season's last night**: a red flag warning in the morning; from dusk dry lightning all round and no rain, and
  a big smoke far off in plain view of your lookout. Marion is not on the night desk tonight, someone else is, and
  "the District" on the radio says to let it go. Call it in and the crews go and the campers in its way come out,
  and he comes up to your door. Let it go and he thanks you at dawn and leaves your lookout be, and a camper in its
  way is not found. The District knows either way: the evaluation, Marion's word in the morning and the next season's
  first mail go by it. Which is right is never said.
- **What the story left on the mountain**: the papers it is found in and two places to go. Hollow Peak, the lookout
  closed in 1979, its footings on a summit some way off, the District's notice on the one leg still standing and
  under where its cab fell a chest with the lookout's log of that summer; Marion says where it is, and the log says
  where the planting is, a quarter mile east below the ridge: rows of spruce, the oldest giants now, something hung
  by each, and a ledger on a lectern among black candles, its last page this world's own (each hiker or keeper
  taken is a new sapling there, with their name). The Board of Review's summary of the Hollow Fire comes up with a
  crate from Marion's archive; a lookout left in a hurry has a clipping from 1980 in a barrel in its cab.
- **The radio used against you**: calls from no one, and a player's own voice played back.
- **What is left** where someone was taken, and their journal: each written its own way, with a page for their last
  night (the weather, where they said they were, the last thing the lookout told them, the whistling if they heard it).
- **The flowing sleep**.
